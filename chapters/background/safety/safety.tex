An important part of ensuring safe reactor operation is making sure safety-related transient events do not disrupt safe reactor operations.
These transients are caused by \glspl{PIE}, which are defined by the \gls{IAEA} in the \gls{IAEA} Safety Standard Series No. NS-R-1 \cite{international2000iaea}.
Of particular importance are \glspl{PIE} which lead to \glspl{DBA}.
Although No. SSR-2/1 has superceded No. NS-R-1, the \glspl{PIE} and \glspl{DBA} for \glspl{MSR} have not changed \cite{international2016iaea}.
Although there are not specific events listed for \glspl{MSR}, guidance is provided on what sort of events are of interest.

These events include equipment failures, such as pumps failing or pipes rupturing; human error, such as improper maintenance or faulty operator actions; external events, such as an earthquake; and combinations of events.
For combinations of events, the \gls{IAEA} specifies that these events should make sense in combination and in the time frame modeled.
For example, if a particular event lasts a long time or has a long lasting effect, then that event could possibly overlap with another unrelated event.
Alternatively, events which are not independent of each other could potentially occur at the same time.



Safety-related transients for \glspl{MSR} include \gls{ULOHS}, \gls{ULOF}, \gls{UTOP}, and \gls{FSOC} \cite{li2015transient, daher2022improvement}.
An \gls{ULOHS} transient is when the heat exchanger experiences a failure, possibly caused by a pump failure in the intermediate loop, which causes a loss of cooling of the reactor.
For the \gls{MSFR}, Brovchenko et al. have shown that slow \gls{ULOHS} transients that take place over a minute or longer minimize the increase in fuel salt temperature \cite{brovchenko2013design}.
An {ULOF} transient is when the flow rate in the primary loop becomes zero, caused by pump failure in the primary loop.
An \gls{UTOP} transient is a reactivity insertion event, which could be caused by various means, such as removing poisons, adding fissile material, or temperature changes.
A \gls{FSOC} transient is when the fuel salt temperature decreases, which could be caused by an increased heat removal rate from the heat exchanger.

These safety-related transients are important for the current work, as the \gls{MSR} specific \gls{DNP} group parameter fits will impact the results of these transients.
Depending on the properties of the specific reactor, which affect the group fits, the static \gls{DNP} group parameter transient simulation may generate overly or insufficiently conservative results.
