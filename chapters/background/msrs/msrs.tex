Liquid-fueled \glspl{MSR} are nuclear reactors which have the fissile material dissolved in a circulating molten salt. 
This fuel salt circulates through the in-core region, where most fissions occur, and through external piping in the ex-core region, where chemical processing occurs, after which it re-enters the in-core region.
The salt is both the fuel and coolant in an \gls{MSR}, as the ex-core region also contains the heat exchanger.

There are many different \gls{MSR} designs in the literature, as shown in Table \ref{tab:msr-designs} \cite{cheng2014development}.
These designs feature a circulating salt containing fissile material.
In particular, the circulating fuel salt allows for online chemical reprocessing, which causes depletion to vary spatially, leads to movement of \glspl{DNP}, and requires close coupling of thermal-hydraulics to neutronics for realistic modeling.

Although these studies incorporate the effects of online chemical reprocessing on depletion, they do not explicitly model the spatial effect on depletion.
Instead, a simple approximation is used to handle the in-core and ex-core regions while accounting for the entire primary salt volume.
The reason why simulations do not include fully resolved spatial resolution in depletion calculations is due to the computational cost.
