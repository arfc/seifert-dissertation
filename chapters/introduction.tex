
One of the generation IV reactor designs is the liquid fueled molten salt reactor, or \gls{MSR} \cite{kelly_generation_2014}. 
\Glspl{MSR} are advanced reactors which are intended to improve safety and efficiency over previous reactor designs.
\Glspl{MSR}, instead of a solid fuel, use a liquid fuel composed of a molten salt mixed with some fissile nuclide, such as $^{235}$U.
There are a wide variety of benefits to \glspl{MSR}: high operating temperature, low operating pressure, and high thermal conductivity and capacity of the salts.
Other benefits include a high coefficient of thermal expansion for negative temperature coefficient of reactivity, high resource utilization through higher burn-up, reduced cost of fabricating and transporting fuel elements, and the removal of fission products to improve the neutron economy \cite{serp_molten_2014}.

\Glspl{MSR} have several barriers to deployment which must be addressed before they are commercialized.
Corrosion and material degradation is an issue for \glspl{MSR}, as the high temperature salts are highly corrosive.
Another barrier to deployment is in reactor safety, security, and safeguards by design, as \glspl{MSR} have distinct designs compared to traditional light water reactors.
To properly design \glspl{MSR}, we must have models which are able to simulate them correctly.
One specific phenomena which is of critical importance in \glspl{MSR} are the \glspl{DNP}.


\Glspl{DNP} are nuclides which decay via $\beta^-$ decay, producing a daughter, called an emitter, which emits a neutron \cite{bank2000jef}.
Properly modeling the \glspl{DNP} in reactors is important, otherwise simulations may predict incorrect power responses, reactivity worth, and reactor controllability \cite{das1993importance, das1977kinetics, lane1958fluid}.
The \glspl{DNP} are typically not modeled explicitly in simulations, but are instead represented using \gls{DNP} group parameters, which are intended to have the same physical effect at a much lower computational cost.

The progress in \gls{DNP} modeling has progressed rapidly thanks to experimental data available for individual \gls{DNP}, which allowed for more accurate group parameter formulation.
However, these formulations of \gls{DNP} group parameters do not account for certain physics unique to \gls{MSR}.
Although there has also been much analysis on \glspl{DNP} in \glspl{MSR}, that work has also not investigated this gap \cite{zhang2009analysis, zuo2022flow, lee2022transient, shi2021gen, kupriyanov2022effective}.
Most of these studies investigate movement of \glspl{DNP} during transients, not how the fuel movement and chemical reprocessing affects the formulation of \gls{DNP} group parameters.
However, to have accurate transient models, it is necessary that the \gls{DNP} group parameters are accurate.
Thus, a new step forward for group parameter development is for \gls{DNP} group parameters to accurately reflect the physical phenomena in \glspl{MSR}.

This physical phenomena can be incorporated at varying levels of fidelity, with associated differences in cost and accuracy.
The least accurate and fastest is a 0D scaled model.
This model neglects time dependence and decay chains while scaling the flux and chemical removal rates based on the salt fractions in different regions of the primary loop.
The intermediate approach is a 0D flow model.
This model accounts for time dependence and decay chains, while applying the flux and chemical removal rates only in the relevant parts of the primary loop.
Finally, the most accurate and slowest approach is a spatially resolved model.
This model accounts for time and space dependence over the entire primary loop.

The preliminary results of this work cover important \glspl{DNP}, sensitivity studies of the \gls{DNP} group parameters, and verification of the 0D scaled model.
The results show that the total delayed neutron yield and average half-life values are within uncertainty bounds between the raw \gls{DNP} data and the \gls{DNP} group parameters.
This verifies the process of turning \gls{DNP} data into \gls{DNP} group parameters.
The sensitivity studies reveal that each \gls{DNP} can multiple groups, not only the ones that are closest to them.
Finally, the verification with results from the literature reveals a main weakness of the 0D scaled model: it does not include pulse irradiation data.
This results in a poor set of \gls{DNP} group parameters for short-lived groups.

The proposed work includes evaluation of the improved \gls{DNP} group parameters, evaluation of data, uncertainty quantification, group spectra generation, additional model development, and kinetics simulations.
