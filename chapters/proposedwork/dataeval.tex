The use and handling of the various data has a direct impact on the \gls{DNP} group parameters.
These impacts can be caused by the data source, time resolution of the fission yield, number of \glspl{DNP} included in the model, and energy of the incident neutrons used to irradiate the sample.
The data source includes the source of cross-section data, fission yields, emission probabilities, half-lives, and other associated data used to generate the \gls{DNP} group parameters.
The time resolution of the fission yield is the difference between using a cumulative fission yield and a time-resolved depletion chain for evaluating \gls{DNP} concentrations.
The number of \glspl{DNP} is important because modeling more \glspl{DNP} is more computationally expensive for the spatially resolved model, so minimizing this term without affect \gls{DNP} group generation is of interest.
Finally, the incident neutron energy spectra is used to study the most effective way of organizing the \gls{DNP} group parameters with regards to energy, such as using the median or mean energy.

\subsection{Fission Yield Time Resolution}
The time resolution for when the \glspl{DNP} are formed is unimportant for stationary, saturation irradiation \gls{DNP} group parameter formulations, which means such analyses can use the cumulative fission yields rather than a full depletion simulation.
However, for calculation of group parameters with circulation taken into account, the time resolution of the formation of the \glspl{DNP} may be important to model.
This is an important consideration, as if using the cumulative fission yield generates the same group parameters as the full depletion simulation, then the computational cost can be greatly reduced.
It is also useful because the spatially resolved method is much more efficient using the cumulative fission yield rather than full spatially resolved depletion.

Before comparing the methods for the circulating system, they are first compared for a stationary system.
The \gls{DNP} group parameters should align for both methods in a stationary system.
If they do not, then that would mean either there is a fault in the implementation or that this approach, which is common in the literature, may not be an accurate way of treating the \glspl{DNP}.

To evaluate the efficacy of using the cumulative fission yield compared to depletion, a direct comparison will be made between the group parameters generated using each method.
The same dataset, \gls{ENDF}/B-VIII.0, will be used for both to ensure there are minimal differences.
The \gls{DNP} group parameters will be generated using each method for a variety of residence times, so as to capture the time-dependent effects in higher resolution.

If the \gls{DNP} group parameters are identical for all residence times, then this will greatly reduce the computational costs and will be implemented.
If they have non-negligible differences, then the more costly method will continue to be used.

\subsection{Number of DNP}
The number of \gls{DNP} which must be tracked is of interest, particularly for the spatially resolved flow model.
The 0D flow model computational cost increases negligibly with more \gls{DNP} to track, while the spatially resolved flow model requires much more memory and operations in order to track each new \gls{DNP} over both space and time.
To improve the computational efficiency of the spatially resolved flow model, minimizing the number of \glspl{DNP} is of interest.
However, if the 0D flow model is capable of generating \gls{DNP} group parameters which have negligible difference to those of the spatially resolved flow model, then the minimization of the number of \glspl{DNP} is less useful.
For potential future analyses which could include spatially resolved reprocessing and other effects, it is still of interest to optimize the spatially resolved flow model.

The impact of each \gls{DNP} is given in Equation \eqref{eq:delnu-net-nuc}, replicated here:
\begin{equation}
    \bar{\nu}_{d, i} = P_{n, i} \lambda_i N_{i, 0}.
    \nonumber
\end{equation}
Dividing $\bar{\nu}_{d, i}$ by the total delayed neutron yield, $\bar{\nu}_d$, provides the relative contribution of each \gls{DNP} to the total delayed neutron yield.
The \glspl{DNP} with the most impact can be modeled preferentially to determine the minimum number of \glspl{DNP} to model with negligible changes to the \gls{DNP} group parameters.

Comparisons will be made between group parameters generated with varying numbers of the most important \glspl{DNP}.
A full sensitivity study will be conducted to determine a reasonable number of \glspl{DNP} to include in the spatially resolved flow model.

\subsection{Incident Neutron Energy Spectra}
\label{sec:indidentspectra}
The incident neutron energy spectrum directly affects the \gls{DNP} group parameters.
However, it would be exceedingly computationally expensive to generate new \gls{DNP} group parameters for every slightly different incident neutron energy spectrum.
Some resolution is required, so it is of interest to determine how to best categorize the \gls{DNP} group parameters for different energy spectra.

This sensitivity study will investigate different levels of fidelity to organize the energy spectra: fine spectra, middling spectra, coarse spectra, and single value spectra.
The approach will use the 0D flow model with the only changes being to the neutron source energy spectra.
The fine, middling, and coarse spectra will contain the full data of the incident neutron energy spectra, but with different sized energy bins.
The single value spectra contain several categories: neutron energy classification, mean energy, and median energy.
The neutron energy classification is given in Table \ref{tab:neutronenergyspectra}, recreated from \cite{l2003nuclear}.
The study will also investigate hand-crafted spectra where the mean and median remain unchanged while the spectrum itself changes.

    \centering
    \begin{tabular}{cc}
    \hline
        Category & Energy Range \\
    \hline
      Cold   & $< 0.003$ $[eV]$ \\
      Thermal & $0.003 - 0.4$ $[eV]$ \\
      Epithermal & $0.4 - 100$ $[eV]$ \\

      Fast & $0.2 - 10$ $[MeV]$ \\

This sensitivity study will provide an idea of what level of detail is necessary in the incident neutron energy spectrum when generating the \gls{DNP} group parameters.
It is anticipated that organizing by category or by mean energy should be sufficient to categorize the neutron energy spectra for most cases.
For cases where it is not applicable, a more robust approach can be selectively used.
