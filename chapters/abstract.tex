\Glspl{MSR} have garnered interest since the Generation IV International Forum in the early 2000s, where the \gls{MSR} was designated as one of six advanced reactors of interest.
A unique consideration in \glspl{MSR} are \glspl{DNP}, which move from areas of high importance in the core to areas of lower importance in the core and ex-core regions.
This causes the effective delayed neutron fraction to decrease, leading to changes in reactivity based on the salt flow rate.
\glspl{DNP} are important for modeling and simulation of \glspl{MSR}.

Many studies have investigated how the effective delayed neutron fraction changes based on the movement of fuel salt in \glspl{MSR}.
These studies do not account for the effect of fuel salt movement and reprocessing on the \gls{DNP} group parameters used in the transient simulations.
The effect of neglecting these \gls{MSR}-specific phenomena is reduced accuracy in the \gls{DNP} group parameters.

These phenomena are important to consider, as simulations use the \gls{DNP} group parameters to enable safety, security, and safeguards by design.
By using \gls{DNP} group parameters with greater accuracy, models can more accurately reflect physical phenomena.
Having greater accuracy in predictive models is key for developing an advanced reactor pipeline.

The \gls{DNP} group parameters for \glspl{MSR} are resolved using the microscopic, or aggregate or summation, method.
This method entails using individual \gls{DNP} data to reconstruct the \gls{DNP} groups by summing them together in simulated irradiation experiments.
Pulse irradiation experiments occur at very short time scales relative to \gls{MSR} physical phenomena and are used to generate the short lived \gls{DNP} group parameters.
Saturation, or infinite, irradiation experiments occur at time scales several times longer than the longest \gls{DNP} half life and are used to generate the longer lived \gls{DNP} group parameters.
Because the saturation irradiation lasts on the order of minutes, the thermal-hydraulic and chemical effects in \glspl{MSR} have a non-negligible impact.
Therefore, the longer lived \gls{DNP} group parameters will vary, while the shorter lived \gls{DNP} group parameters will remain the same.

A non-linear least-squares solver is used to generate an improved set of \gls{DNP} group parameters.
A specific \gls{DNP} group parameter set is proposed for a particular set of conditions in the \gls{MSRE}.
Verification is conducted via comparison with total delayed neutron yields and average half-lives from the literature.

The results demonstrate the validity of these \gls{MSR} phenomena-inclusive \gls{DNP} group parameters.
The methodology used to generate these parameters can be replicated for other \gls{MSR} conditions, enabling the formation of more accurate sets of \gls{DNP} group parameters for \glspl{MSR}.
This improves the accuracy of \gls{MSR} simulations, which is important for safety, security, and safeguards by design as well as for developing an advanced reactor pipeline.
Proposed work includes the following: development and evaluation of a more sophisticated model for generating the \gls{DNP} group parameters,
validation via comparison with the \gls{MSRE} during pump start-up and coast-down,
generation of polynomial fits for various parameters used in the \gls{DNP} group generation,
generation of \gls{DNP} group spectra,
uncertainty quantification,
analysis of the flux shape effect on \gls{DNP} group parameters,
and evaluation of various datasets on \gls{DNP} group parameters.
