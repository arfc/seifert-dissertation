\Glspl{MSR} have garnered interest since the Generation IV International Forum in the early 2000s, where the \gls{MSR} was designated as one of six advanced reactors of interest.
A unique consideration in \glspl{MSR} are \glspl{DNP}, which move from areas of high neutronic importance in the core to areas of lower importance in the core and ex-core regions.
This causes the effective delayed neutron fraction to decrease, impacting reactor behavior in multiple ways.

Accurate models and simulations of \glspl{DNP} are important for \gls{MSR} safety and design.

Many studies have investigated how the effective delayed neutron fraction changes based on the fuel salt movement in \glspl{MSR}.
The movement of the \glspl{DNP} is modeled using \gls{DNP} groups, typically six groups, with each group $k$ consisting of a decay constant $\lambda_k$ and yield of delayed neutrons per fission event $\bar{\nu}_{d, k}$.
However, these studies do not account for the effects of fuel salt movement and chemical removal rates on the \gls{DNP} group parameters, $\lambda_k$ and $\bar{\nu}_{d, k}$, used in the transient simulations.
Neglecting these \gls{MSR}-specific phenomena reduces accuracy in the \gls{DNP} group parameters in \gls{MSR} simulations.

Fuel salt recirculation and chemical removal rates are important to consider, as simulations use the \gls{DNP} group parameters to enable safety, security, and safeguards by design.
By using \gls{DNP} group parameters with greater accuracy, models can more accurately reflect physical phenomena.
Having greater accuracy in predictive models is key for successful \gls{MSR} deployment.

This work resolves \gls{DNP} group parameters for \glspl{MSR} using the microscopic method, also known as the aggregate or summation method.
This method entails using individual \gls{DNP} data to reconstruct the \gls{DNP} groups by summing the delayed neutron count rate contribution of each \gls{DNP} in simulated irradiation experiments.
Pulse irradiation experiments occur at very short time scales relative to \gls{MSR} physical phenomena and are used to generate the short-lived \gls{DNP} group parameters.
Saturation, or infinite, irradiation experiments occur at time scales several times longer than the longest \gls{DNP} half-life and are used to generate the longer lived \gls{DNP} group parameters.
Because the saturation irradiation lasts on the order of minutes, the thermal-hydraulic and chemical effects in \glspl{MSR} have a non-negligible impact.
Therefore, the longer-lived \glspl{DNP}' group parameters will vary, while the shorter-lived \glspl{DNP}' group parameters will remain constant.

A non-linear least squares solver is used to generate an improved set of \gls{DNP} group parameters.
%A specific \gls{DNP} group parameter set is proposed for a particular set of conditions in the \gls{MSRE}.
The method is verified by comparing the improved \gls{DNP} group parameters to the data used to generate those parameters, as the total delayed neutron yields and average half-lives should be nearly identical.
Verification of the parameters is conducted via comparison with total delayed neutron yields and average half-lives from the literature.

The methodology used to generate these parameters could be altered to account for other \gls{MSR} conditions, such as corrosion and plate-out, enabling the formation of more accurate sets of \gls{DNP} group parameters for \glspl{MSR}.
