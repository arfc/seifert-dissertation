\label{ref:appendixA}

This derivation begins with the Equations \eqref{eq:wilson-R} and \eqref{eq:wilson-n} provided by Wilson and England \cite{wilson2002delayed}:
\begin{equation}
    R(t) = \sum_{k=1}^{K} \bar{\nu}_{d, k} \lambda_k e^{-\lambda_k t},
    \label{eq:wilson-R}
\end{equation}
and,
\begin{equation}
    \dot{n}_d(T, t) = \bar{\nu}_d \int_{0}^{T} F(\tau) R(T-\tau+t) d\tau,
    \label{eq:wilson-n}
\end{equation}
where,
\begin{align}
T &= \mbox{ the length of the fission history [$s$]} \nonumber \\
t &= \mbox{ the decay time from the end of the fission history [$s$]} \nonumber \\
F(\tau) &= \mbox{ the fission rate history [$\frac{fissions}{s}$].} \nonumber
\end{align}
Because the results are normalized to a single delayed neutron, the $\bar{\nu}_d$ term is divided out.

Although the saturation, or infinite, irradiation derivation makes use of assuming the time is infinite, due to the spatial dependence of this problem, the in-core and ex-core timing must be handled properly.
For example, if the fission rate history uses a Fourier decomposition of a square wave, then it would not be possible to extract spatial information from this formulation.
Specifically, it is important where in the primary loop the sample initially exists.
If the sample is initially in the core inlet, then the final portion of the fission rate history would consist purely of decay.
If the sample is initially in the center of the core, then the final portion of the fission rate history would consist of irradiation.

For this derivation, the sample is assumed to exist as a point at the core inlet.
This means the full cycle consists of an in-core residence time of fission followed by an ex-core residence time of decay, after which the cycle repeats until the net irradiation time is met.
The fission rate history is therefore given as:
\begin{align}
    \frac{F(\tau)}{F_0} =& H(\tau) - H(\tau - T) + \sum_{j=1}^J H(\tau - j\cdot \tau_{in} - j\cdot \tau_{ex}) \nonumber \\
    & - H(\tau - j \cdot\tau_{in} - (j-1) \cdot \tau_{ex}),
    \label{eq:pos-square}
\end{align}
where,
\begin{align}
    H =& \text{ the Heaviside function [-]} \nonumber \\
    J =& \text{the minimum number of cycles to meet the irradiation time, $T$ [-]} \nonumber \\
    F_0 =& \text{ the magnitude of the constant fission rate [$\frac{fissions}{s}$].} \nonumber
\end{align}

Plugging this into Equation \eqref{eq:wilson-n} for group $k$ yields:
\begin{align}
\dot{n}_{d, k}(T, t) =& \int_{0}^{T} H(\tau) \bar{\nu}_{d, k} \lambda_k e^{-\lambda_k (T-\tau+t)} d\tau - \int_{0}^{T} H(\tau-T) \bar{\nu}_{d, k} \lambda_k e^{-\lambda_k (T-\tau+t)} d\tau\nonumber \\
& + \int_{0}^{T} \sum_{j=1}^J H(\tau - j\cdot \tau_{in} - j\cdot \tau_{ex}) \bar{\nu}_{d, k} \lambda_k e^{-\lambda_k (T-\tau+t)} d\tau \nonumber\\
& - \int_{0}^{T} \sum_{j=1}^J H(\tau - j \cdot\tau_{in} - (j-1) \cdot \tau_{ex}) \bar{\nu}_{d, k} \lambda_k e^{-\lambda_k (T-\tau+t)} d\tau,
\end{align}
which can be simplified to:
\begin{align}
\frac{\dot{n}_{d, k}(T, t)}{F_0 \bar{\nu}_{d, k} \lambda_k} =& \int_{0}^{T} H(\tau)   e^{-\lambda_k (T-\tau+t)} d\tau - \int_{0}^{T} H(\tau-T)   e^{-\lambda_k (T-\tau+t)} d\tau\nonumber \\
& + \int_{0}^{T} \sum_{j=1}^J H(\tau - j\cdot \tau_{in} - j\cdot \tau_{ex})   e^{-\lambda_k (T-\tau+t)} d\tau \nonumber\\
& - \int_{0}^{T} \sum_{j=1}^J H(\tau - j \cdot\tau_{in} - (j-1) \cdot \tau_{ex}) e^{-\lambda_k (T-\tau+t)} d\tau
\end{align}

Solving the integrals yields:
\begin{align}
\frac{\dot{n}_{d, k}(T, t)}{F_0 \bar{\nu}_{d, k} \lambda_k} =& \frac{1}{\lambda_k} \left( e^{-\lambda_k t} - e^{-\lambda_k (t+T)} \right) + 0 \nonumber \\
& + \sum_{j=1}^J \frac{H(T-(j \cdot \tau_{in} + j \cdot \tau_{ex}))}{\lambda_k} \left( e^{-\lambda_k t} - e^{\lambda_k ((j \cdot \tau_{in} + j \cdot \tau_{ex}) - t - T)} \right) \nonumber\\
& - \sum_{j=1}^J \frac{H(T-(j \cdot \tau_{in} + (j-1) \cdot \tau_{ex}))}{\lambda_k} \left( e^{-\lambda_k t} - e^{\lambda_k ((j \cdot \tau_{in} + (j-1) \cdot \tau_{ex}) - t - T)} \right).
\end{align}
The Heaviside functions in the summations resolve to unity while the cycle is within range of the net irradiation time, and become 0 when the cycle time goes beyond the net irradiation time.
The problem then simplifies to:
\begin{align}
\frac{\dot{n}_{d, k}(T, t)}{F_0 \bar{\nu}_{d, k}} =& \left( e^{-\lambda_k t} - e^{-\lambda_k (t+T)} \right) \nonumber \\
& + \sum_{j=1}^J \left( e^{-\lambda_k t} - e^{\lambda_k ((j \cdot \tau_{in} + j \cdot \tau_{ex}) - t - T)} \right) \nonumber\\
& - \sum_{j=1}^J \left( e^{-\lambda_k t} - e^{\lambda_k ((j \cdot \tau_{in} + (j-1) \cdot \tau_{ex}) - t - T)} \right).
\end{align}

If the net irradiation time is assumed to be infinite, then the delayed neutron count rate becomes:
\begin{align}
\dot{n}_{d, k}(T, t) =& F_0 \bar{\nu}_{d, k} e^{-\lambda_k t},
\label{eq:wilseng-examp}
\end{align}
which matches the result from Wilson and England \cite{wilson2002delayed}.
However, as previously mentioned, this infinite approach will not work for the spatially dependent analysis.
For example, if an in-core residence time is selected which is relatively small compared to the ex-core residence time, Equation \eqref{eq:wilseng-examp} would not vary at all, even though the fission rate history would be significantly different.
Instead, the problem takes on the form:
\begin{align}
\frac{\dot{n}_{d, k}(T, t)}{F_0 \bar{\nu}_{d, k} e^{-\lambda_k t}} =&  1 - e^{-\lambda_k T} + \left( 1-e^{\lambda_k \tau_{ex}} \right) \sum_{j=1}^J e^{\lambda_k (j \cdot \tau_{in} + (j-1)\cdot \tau_{ex}-T)}.
\end{align}
By isolating the delayed neutron count rate and summing over all groups, the finalized equation can be formulated as:
\begin{align}
\dot{n}_{d}(T, t) =& F_0 \sum_{k=1}^K \bar{\nu}_{d, k} e^{-\lambda_k t} - \bar{\nu}_{d, k} e^{-\lambda_k t} e^{-\lambda_k T} \\
& + \bar{\nu}_{d, k} e^{-\lambda_k t} \left( 1-e^{\lambda_k \tau_{ex}} \right) \sum_{j=1}^J e^{\lambda_k (j \cdot \tau_{in} + (j-1)\cdot \tau_{ex}-T)},
\end{align}
which can be rewritten as:
\begin{align}
\dot{n}_{d}(T, t) =& F_0 \sum_{k=1}^K \bar{\nu}_{d, k} e^{-\lambda_k t} \left(1 - e^{-\lambda_k T} +  \left( 1-e^{\lambda_k \tau_{ex}} \right) \sum_{j=1}^J e^{\lambda_k (j \cdot \tau_{in} + (j-1)\cdot \tau_{ex}-T)} \right).
\end{align}
If the in-core residence time is zero, then the delayed neutron count rate goes to zero, which is the expected behavior.
