Moltres is an open-source \gls{MOOSE} application that simulates \glspl{MSR} by solving the multi-group neutron diffusion and thermal-hydraulics equations \cite{lindsay_introduction_2018}.
This work uses Moltres to simulate the \gls{MSRE} with different \gls{DNP} group parameters.
Moltres also validates the updated \gls{DNP} group parameters in this work.

The \gls{MSRE} experimental data can be used for validation, and was previously modeled with Moltres \cite{lindsay_introduction_2018}.
The \gls{MSRE} core consists of flow through channels, simplifying its modeling compared to open-pool designs due to the restricted flow path.
Since the initial demonstration, Park et al. have noted general agreement in results, attributing differences to simplifications and assumptions in the geometry \cite{park2022verification}.
Additionally, Park has published a dissertation detailing 1-D, 2-D, and 3-D neutronic models of the \gls{MSRE} \cite{park_advancements_2025}.

Moltres can simulate transients using the \gls{MSR}-specific \gls{DNP} group parameters, and it can generate updated parameters with higher fidelity than the 0D method.
Moltres couples three physical phenomena to model an \gls{MSR}: neutron flux, \glspl{DNP}, and thermal-hydraulics.

\subsubsection{Flux Modeling}

Neutron flux modeling determines the energy of neutrons interacting with the sample and the number of interactions.
It determines the fission rate and the resulting fission product concentrations, including \glspl{DNP}.
The time-dependent multi-group neutron diffusion equation appears in Equation \eqref{eq:mg-n-diff}, recreated here:

\begin{equation}
    \begin{split}
    \frac{1}{\nu_g} \frac{\partial \phi_g}{\partial t} = & \; \nabla \cdot D_g \nabla \phi_g + \sum_{g' \neq g}^G \Sigma_{s, g' \rightarrow g} \phi_{g'} \\
    & + (1-\beta) \chi_{p, g} \sum_{g'=1}^{G} (\nu \Sigma_f)_{g'} \phi_{g'}\\
    & \sum_{i=1}^I \chi_{d, g, i} \lambda_i C_i - \Sigma_{a, g} \phi_g - \sum_{g' \neq g}^G \Sigma_{s, g \rightarrow g'} \phi_{g}
    \nonumber
    \end{split}
\end{equation}

Modeling the neutron flux requires accounting for the \glspl{DNP}.
\glspl{DNP} contribute to the neutron flux and possess their own energy spectra, necessitating their inclusion as a separate term.

\subsubsection{Precursor Modeling}

The time dependent \gls{DNP} concentration is given in Equation \eqref{eq:mg-n-diff-Ci} and recreated here:

\begin{equation}
    \frac{\partial C_i}{\partial t} = \beta_i \sum_{g=1}^G (\nu \Sigma_f)_g \phi_g - \lambda_i C_i - \nabla \cdot (C_i \vec{u}) + \nabla \cdot \frac{\nu_T}{Sc_T} \nabla C_i
    \nonumber
\end{equation}

An issue arises with this equation because Moltres generates \gls{MSR}-specific \gls{DNP} group parameters, yet the equation requires predefined \gls{DNP} group parameters.
To generate the \gls{DNP} group parameters, this equation is replaced by \eqref{eq:bate-flow-simple }, recreated here:

\begin{equation}
    \frac{dN_j}{dt} = S(\vec{r}, t) - \lambda_{net, j} N_j(\vec{r}, t) + \nabla \cdot \left( D_{mix} \nabla N_{j} (\vec{r}, t) - \vec{u}(\vec{r}, t) N_{j} (\vec{r}, t) \right)
    \nonumber
\end{equation}
Specifically, the solution to this problem is to use spatially resolved depletion over the irradiation simulation; instead of tracking concentrations of \gls{DNP} groups, the simulation tracks concentrations of the individual \glspl{DNP}.

\subsubsection{Thermal-Hydraulic Modeling}

Moltres offers time-dependent, incompressible, buoyancy-driven flow using Equations \eqref{eq:momemtum} and \eqref{eq:divergence} recreated from \cite{park_advancements_2025}:
\begin{equation}
\begin{split}
    \rho \frac{\partial \vec{u}}{\partial t} &=
    -\rho (\vec{u}
    \cdot \nabla) \vec{u} - \nabla p + \mu \nabla^2 \vec{u}
    + \rho \alpha \vec{g} \left(T - T_{\text{ref}} \right)
    \label{eq:momemtum},
\end{split}
\end{equation}
\begin{equation}
\begin{split}
    \nabla \cdot \vec{u} &= 0,
    \label{eq:divergence}
\end{split}
\end{equation}
where,
\begin{align}
    \rho =& \text{ fluid density} \nonumber \\
    p =& \text{ pressure} \nonumber \\
    \mu =& \text{ dynamic viscosity} \nonumber \\
    \alpha =& \text{ coefficient of thermal expansion} \nonumber \\
    \vec{g} =& \text{ gravitational force vector} \nonumber \\
    T =& \text{ fluid temperature} \nonumber \\
    T_{\text{ref}} =& \text{ reference temperature at which the nominal}
    \nonumber \\
    &\text{ density is provided.} \nonumber
    \nonumber
\end{align}

The temperature distribution is given by Equation \eqref{eq:temp} recreated from \cite{park_advancements_2025}, which shows the temperature advection-diffusion as:

\begin{equation}
\begin{split}
    \rho c_{p} \frac{\partial T}{\partial t} =& - \rho c_p \vec{u}
    \cdot \nabla T + \nabla \cdot \left(k \nabla T \right) + Q_f - Q_s,
    \label{eq:temp}
\end{split}
\end{equation}
and by Equation \eqref{eq:heat-source} recreated from \cite{park_advancements_2025}, which gives the fission heat source term as:
\begin{equation}
\begin{split}
    Q_f =& \sum^G_{g=1} \epsilon_g \Sigma_g^f \phi_g,
    \label{eq:heat-source}
\end{split}
\end{equation}
where,
\begin{align}
    c_p =& \text{ specific heat capacity of molten salt} \nonumber \\
    k =& \text{ effective thermal conductivity of molten salt} \nonumber \\
    Q_f =& \text{ fission heat source} \nonumber \\
    \epsilon_g =& \text{ average fission energy released by neutrons in group
    $g$,} \nonumber \\
    Q_s =& \text{ heat sink/removal.} \nonumber
\end{align}

With the combination of the flux modeling, precursor modeling, and thermal-hydraulic modeling, Moltres can almost simulate \glspl{MSR}.
However, there is one additional \gls{MSR} phenomena to complete the model.
This phenomena is the flow of the fuel salt between the in-core to ex-core regions.

\subsubsection{Ex-core and In-core Coupling}

Moltres treats the ex-core region as a tightly-coupled 1D pipe geometry with coupling using Picard iterations and the inlet/outlet boundary conditions.
The specific equations to treat the variables of interest in this geometry can be derived from their full forms.
For example, the flux and eddy viscosity are zero in the ex-core region, leading to Equation \eqref{eq:prec-conc-ex }, which describes the precursor concentration in the ex-core region as:
\begin{equation}
    \frac{\partial C_i}{\partial t} = - \lambda_i C_i - u_x \frac{\partial C_i}{\partial x}.
    \label{eq:prec-conc-ex}
\end{equation}
