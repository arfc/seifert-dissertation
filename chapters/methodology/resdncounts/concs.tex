The OpenMC model uses a fixed-source problem on a three gram spherical sample of a given fissile target.
A uniformly distributed source is placed inside the target, and a vacuum boundary condition is used at the edge of the sphere.
The sample must have a sufficient volume such that good statistics are achieved, but a volume that is too large can increase self-shielding and/or self-multiplication.
This can be adjusted by modifying the density, where a more dense target increases self-shielding and self-multiplication.
A less dense target reduces the probability of fission, which worsens the statistics for the solve.

The fixed-source used in this model is made up entirely of neutrons at a given energy, rather than a spectrum.
This energy affects the fission cross sections, transmutation cross sections, and fission yields.
Traditionally, tabulated group parameter data is formatted for a single energy or a range of energies.
However, the macroscopic experiments, discussed in Section \ref{sec:measurementofdnps}, are performed on a reactor which has a neutron energy spectrum, not mono-energetic neutrons.
This could be worked around by modeling a reactor in OpenMC and using a k-eigenvalue solve to generate a neutron energy spectra.
However, for this work, the effects of the spectra are considered to be sufficiently small in scale that they do not need to be included.
There are also photons which can generate delayed neutrons through photofission, though these are not investigated in this work \cite{kinlaw2007delayed}.

The distribution of the neutron source does not precisely describe the actual experimental process, where neutrons irradiate the sample from around the sample rather than inside.
However, having the source evenly distributed removes concerns of self-shielding from the problem.
The exact spatial locations where fission events occur within the sample should cause negligible changes in the concentrations of nuclides, which is the output from the OpenMC simulation.
Assuming a density of 10 $\frac{g}{cc}$, then the spherical sample has a radius of approximately 0.4 $cm$.
The spatial resolution of the concentrations in such a small sample should not be significant.
Additionally, the concentrations extracted from the OpenMC simulation are spatially collapsed in the post-processing, so the spatial effect of a uniformly distributed source serves only to reduce any self-shielding effects which could reduce the statistics and cause more transmutation than expected.

After running the OpenMC simulation, the concentrations are collected from the depletion results for the final time step of irradiation.
This final time step of concentrations can then be passed along to the other scripts.
These other scripts handle conversion of concentrations to delayed neutron counts.
