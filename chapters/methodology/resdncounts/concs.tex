The concentrations from the 0D scaled model are calculated using Equation \ref{eq:cfy-conc}, while the concentrations in the spatially resolved model can be calculated using Equation \ref{eq:bate-flow-simple}.
The 0D flow model will use OpenMC to handle calculation of the concentrations over time.

The 0D flow model will use a fixed-source problem on a three-gram spherical sample of a fissile target in OpenMC.
A uniformly distributed source will be placed inside the target with a vacuum boundary condition at the edge of the sphere.
The sample must have a sufficiently large volume to ensure a high number of fissions, but excessive volume increases self-shielding and self-multiplication.
Adjusting the density mitigates this issue: higher density increases self-shielding and self-multiplication, while lower density reduces fission probability and worsens statistical accuracy.
By using a uniformly distributed neutron source within the sample, these effects are lessened.
Additionally, since the fission counts can be taken directly from OpenMC tallies, self-multiplication and self-shielding can be accounted for directly without introducing error.

The fixed-source in this model will consist entirely of neutrons at a single energy, rather than a spectrum.
For the proposed analyses, only thermal fission of $^{235}$U is studied.
This energy dictates fission cross sections, transmutation cross sections, and fission yields.
Traditionally, tabulated group parameter data is formatted for a single energy or energy range, such as fast or thermal.
However, macroscopic experiments, discussed in Section \ref{sec:measurementofdnps}, use reactors with varying neutron energy spectra, not mono-energetic neutrons.
This could be addressed by modeling a reactor in OpenMC and using a k-eigenvalue solve to generate a neutron energy spectrum.
This analysis is considered outside the current scope, but is suggested as future work in Section \ref{sec:future-work}.
%However, this work assumes the spectral effects are sufficiently small to exclude.
Photons can also generate delayed neutrons through photofission, though this is also considered outside the current scope \cite{kinlaw2007delayed}.
There are also delayed neutrons from photons through non-fissile material, known as photoneutrons, also outside the current scope \cite{keckler2021photoneutron}.

The neutron source distribution does not replicate the experimental process, where neutrons irradiate the sample externally rather than internally.
However, an evenly distributed source eliminates self-shielding concerns.
The exact spatial locations of fission events within the sample should negligibly affect the nuclide concentrations, which are the outputs of the OpenMC simulation.
With a density of 10 $g \cdot cm^{-3}$, the spherical sample has a radius of approximately 0.4 $cm$.
