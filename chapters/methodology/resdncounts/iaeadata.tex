After receiving the concentrations from OpenMC, the scripts determine the concentration of each nuclide as a function of time during the measurement period.
The concentrations are calculated based on half-life, as shown in Equation \eqref{eq:radiodec}, where the concentration as a function of time is:
\begin{equation}
    N(t) = N_0 e^{-\lambda t}.
    \label{eq:radiodec}
\end{equation}

This simple equation does not account for decay chains between nuclides.
An alternative approach uses repeated zero-power depletion simulations in OpenMC to generate concentrations as a function of time, incorporating decay chains.
These concentrations are available at coarse time steps and linearly interpolated using SciPy, allowing the scripts to access more accurate concentrations at any time after irradiation \cite{virtanen2020scipy}.
This approach only models delayed neutrons generated during decay, as it is beyond the scope of this work to model other particles \cite{chen2002rigorous}.
Section \ref{sec:decaychaindaughters} compares these two approaches.

The concentrations are combined with Equation \eqref{eq:mdnp-dn}, recreated here:
\begin{equation}
\dot{n}_d(t) = \epsilon \sum_{k=1}^{K} P_{n, k} \lambda_k N_{k}(t).
\nonumber
\end{equation}
The efficiency term, $\epsilon$, is set to 1 in this work, as the simulated method includes every produced delayed neutron in the count rate.
The contributions of each \gls{DNP} over time are summed to generate the delayed neutron count rate.
