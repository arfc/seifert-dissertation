After receiving the concentrations from OpenMC, \gls{MoSDeN} determines the concentration of each nuclide as a function of time during the measurement period.
The concentrations are calculated based on half-life, as shown in Equation \eqref{eq:radiodec}, where the concentration as a function of time is:
\begin{equation}
    N(t) = N_0 e^{-\lambda t}.
    \label{eq:radiodec}
\end{equation}

Equation \eqref{eq:radiodec} shows the approach used in the 0D scaled model, and does not account for decay chains between nuclides.
However, the general form of the equation can still be used, but with inclusion of the various decay chains and branching ratios.
A simple way of implementing decay with the decay chain is using zero-power depletion simulations in OpenMC.
This is used in the 0D flow model, though the 0D flow model also offers the option of using Equation \eqref{eq:radiodec}.
These concentrations are available at coarse time steps and linearly interpolated using SciPy, allowing \gls{MoSDeN} to access more accurate concentrations at any time after irradiation \cite{virtanen2020scipy}.
The specific time steps at which concentrations are written will be investigated after development of the 0D flow model.
%This approach only models delayed neutrons generated during decay, as it is beyond the scope of this work to model other particles \cite{chen2002rigorous}.

The concentrations are combined with Equation \eqref{eq:mdnp-dn}, recreated here:
\begin{equation}
\dot{n}_d(t) = \epsilon \sum_{k=1}^{K} P_{n, k} \lambda_k N_{k}(t).
\nonumber
\end{equation}
The efficiency term, $\epsilon$, is set to 1 in this work, as the simulated method includes every produced delayed neutron in the count rate.
The contributions of each \gls{DNP} over time are summed to generate the delayed neutron count rate.
