To create tabulated \gls{DNP} group parameter data in a reactor agnostic manner, the manner of tabulation needs to be reactor agnostic as well.
At the same time, a large number of tabulation parameters to fully detail the flow regime is not computationally feasible.
Instead, the flow-related tabulation uses only three parameters: a mixing rate ($\lambda_{mix}$), an in-core average residence time ($\tau_{in}$), and an ex-core residence time ($\tau_{ex}$).

This approach captures the advection of the fuel salt around the primary loop as well as an approximation of turbulent and diffusive mixing.
The terms are based on the averaged behavior from a control volume of fuel salt, so thermal-hydraulic effects such as vortices would be treated as changes in residence times and mixing rate.
This method allows an approximate fitting for any circulating fuel reactor geometry, where all that is required is generating the relevant parameters for accessing the tabulated data.

The other tabulation required is for the reprocessing, which can vary in which fission products are removed and their reprocessing rates.
Refueling is not considered in this work, as for the short time frames analyzed, the effects of adding additional fuel are considered to be negligible.
Additionally, since the saturation irradiation time frame is on the order of multiple half lives of the longest lived \gls{DNP}, this means a maximum irradiation time of five minutes is required to reach equilibrium \glspl{DNP} concentrations.
This means that elements which have cycle times on the order of months to years are likely to have negligible changes and do not need to be treated.
Elements with changes on the order of seconds to days will have a sufficient contribution such that their inclusion is needed to generate accurate data.

The \gls{MSBR} reprocessing scheme is used as a base in this work, shown in Table \ref{tab:msbr_cycle_times}.
The rare earths, seminoble metals, volatile fluorides, discard, and higher nuclides are likely negligible for the time scales studied in this work.
However, to ensure that they are negligible, the effects of these larger cycle time terms are studied in Section \ref{sec:large-tcyc}.
Additionally, to better understand how the reprocessing rates affect the group parameters, the cycle times are scaled up and down in Section \ref{sec:repr-scale}.

Overall, the tabulations which are used are incident neutron energy, target fissile nuclide, in-core residence time, ex-core residence time, mixing rate, reprocessing scheme, and reprocessing scaling vector.
Because each unique tabulation is an extra dimension of data that must be collected, it becomes increasingly expensive to compute all of these tabulated results.
In this work, the reprocessing scheme uses the \gls{MSBR} reprocessing scheme and a single scaling scalar, rather than a vector containing scaling values for each element.
Additionally, for the simplified model, discussed in Section \ref{sec:simple-model}, the mixing rate is not used, which also reduces the net computational cost of generating the tabulated data.
The incident neutron energies used are 0.0253 $eV$ and 1 $MeV$.
For the residence times, a study is carried out in Section \ref{sec:res-time-var} to determine the minimum number of residence times and their values to capture general trends.
