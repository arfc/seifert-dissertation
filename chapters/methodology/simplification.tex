Generating \gls{DNP} group parameter data in a reactor-agnostic manner requires a reactor-agnostic generation process.
Fully detailing the flow regime with a large number of parameters is computationally infeasible.
Instead, the flow is simplified to two parameters: an in-core average residence time, $\tau_{in}$, and an ex-core residence time, $\tau_{ex}$.

This approach captures fuel salt advection around the primary loop and approximates turbulent and diffusive mixing.
The terms derive from the averaged behavior of a fuel salt control volume, treating thermal-hydraulic effects, such as vortices, as changes in residence times.
This method allows for approximate fitting of any circulating fuel reactor, requiring only the generation of relevant parameters for determining the associated \gls{DNP} group parameters.

Online refueling is not of interest in this work as it only affects the fission-weighting of the \gls{DNP} group parameters, as discussed in Section \ref{sec:calc-group-param}.
For example, if the irradiated sample is $^{235}$U and the refuel is $^{239}$Pu, then the addition of $^{239}$Pu to the system would only change the fission-weighting and not the actual \gls{DNP} concentrations from each fissile nuclide.
Additionally, even if the same fissile nuclide is added, this could simply be treated as many identical individual samples circulating in the system.
Similarly, if chemical removal removes some amount of fissile nuclide, that is also not important for this analysis.
This is because the \gls{DNP} group parameters are normalized per fission, so the only differences from adding or removing fissile nuclides are on the fission-weighting.

An irradiation time of approximately seven minutes suffices to reach equilibrium \glspl{DNP} concentrations, as the saturation time frame is on the order of multiple half-lives of the longest-lived \gls{DNP}.
Elements with cycle times on the order of months to years exhibit negligible changes, as discussed in Section \ref{sec:large-tcyc}.
Elements with changes on the order of seconds to days contribute sufficiently and must be included for accurate data generation.

The \gls{MSBR} reprocessing scheme serves as the basis for analyzing the most extreme cycle times in this work.
This is because the \gls{MSBR} presents very large chemical removal rates, providing useful conservative estimates as to the impact these large removal rates could have on \gls{DNP} group parameters.
These chemical removal rates are given in Table \ref{tab:msbr_cycle_times}.
Rare earths, semi-noble metals, volatile fluorides, discard, and higher nuclides have small removal rates relative to the time scales of interest for saturation irradiations.
Section \ref{sec:large-tcyc} examines these longer cycle times, the inverse of the removal rates, to confirm their negligible impact.
Section \ref{sec:repr-scale} scales the cycle times to analyze how reprocessing rates influence the group parameters.

With the chemical reprocessing scheme in place, the next step to generate the improved \gls{DNP} group parameters is to decide on the level of fidelity. 
The three levels of fidelity are the 0D scaled model, the 0D flow model, and the spatially resolved model.
The 0D scaled model is completed, the 0D flow model is planned, and the spatially resolved model is a potential future development.

A Monte Carlo sampling approach is used to propagate uncertainties in the various inputs, such as in emission probabilities, half-lives, fission yields, reprocessing terms, or residence times.
The sampling approach simply repeats the process for generating the \gls{DNP} group yields, but with randomly sampled input data.
The data itself must be perturbed, and not the delayed neutron count rate itself.
This is because the uncertainties in the delayed neutron count rate are not independent from time step to time step, but instead they all depend on the underlying data which changes the shape of the delayed neutron count rate at all times.

For generation of the improved \gls{DNP} group parameters, the main goal is to formulate them for individual fissile nuclides.
For some of the analyses presented in this work, the fissions do not come from a single fissile nuclide.
For these analyses, a fission-weighted approach is used, where the \gls{DNP} group parameters are scaled based on the fraction of fissions that lead to those group parameters.
Equations \eqref{eq:nubar-fw} and \eqref{eq:decayconst-fw} show how the fission-weighted \gls{DNP} group parameters can be calculated.
The fission-weighted values can then be multiplied by an importance term to account for the difference between the delayed and prompt neutron fission spectra, as shown in Equations \eqref{eq:yield-fw-import} and \eqref{eq:decay-fw-import} \cite{radaideh2019new}.
