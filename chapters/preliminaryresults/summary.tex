This chapter presented results of the 0D scaled model on thermal fission of $^{235}$U, which demonstrated generation of improved \gls{DNP} group parameters that account for chemical removal in the \gls{MSBR}.
These results consist of analysis of individual \glspl{DNP}' yields, concentrations, and count rates; various sensitivities; and verification of \gls{DNP} group parameters with results in the literature.
The 0D scaled model results show good agreement with the literature for the longer-lived groups, but have poor agreement for the shorter-lived groups, indicating the need for improvements to the model and analysis of \gls{DNP} data.
The sensitivities include analysis of chemical removal rates, time nodes, and nuclear data uncertainties.
These sensitivities are useful for understanding how the chemical removal affects the \gls{DNP} group parameters and analyzing how each group responds to changes in nuclear data based on its uncertainty.

Of the \gls{MSR} physical phenomena considered, only chemical removal has an impact on the 0D scaled model.
The 0D scaled model does not account for decay chains, removing residence time dependence and causing the fission rate scaling to be identical to the \gls{DNP} generation rate scaling.
This causes the in-core and ex-core residence times to not have any effect in the 0D scaled model.
The proposed 0D flow model will be able to model the effects of residence time, as it accounts for time-dependent decays and parasitic absorptions.
Although the 0D scaled model is rapid and enables more simulations, it does not incorporate fuel salt circulation into improved \gls{DNP} group parameter generation and fails to properly model short-lived \gls{DNP} groups.

The failure to model short-lived \gls{DNP} groups may be due to a combination of issues in the fission yields, half-lives, or emission probabilities as well as the lack of pulse irradiation data.
The proposed work will investigate both of these possibilities, as described in Chapter \ref{sec:proposed-work}.
Whether combined via simultaneous solution of a larger matrix or solved separately and then combined, the macroscopic approach indicates pulse irradiation results improve \gls{DNP} group parameters for shorter-lived groups \cite{keepin1957delayed}.
The proposed work will indicate if this holds true for microscopic approach as well.
A simple investigation into the effects of pulse and saturation data is given in Appendix \ref{sec:appendixD}. 
The investigation shows that the saturation irradiation generally captures more \glspl{DNP} than the pulse irradiation, though as fewer \gls{DNP} groups are used, the saturation irradiation has a larger average half-life while the pulse irradiation's is shorter.
There may be contributing factors from the lack of pulse irradiation data and in the \gls{DNP} data used that lead to the short-lived \glspl{DNP} having a larger yield than expected.
The 0D scaled model cannot model pulse irradiations because the cumulative fission yields do not provide accurate concentrations for non-equilibrium concentrations.
Section \ref{sec:irrad-combine} discusses this further.

These preliminary results showed the importance of nuclear data and uncertainties, highlighting $^{86}$Ge as the \gls{DNP} which has the most impact on \gls{DNP} group parameters and the largest nuclear data uncertainty relative to its impact.
The sensitivity analysis showed the importance of the short-lived \glspl{DNP} on the \gls{DNP} group parameters, which also provides further motivation to use a model better able to capture these short-lived \glspl{DNP}.
The 0D flow model will capture these short-lived \glspl{DNP} more effectively and improve the group parameters.
