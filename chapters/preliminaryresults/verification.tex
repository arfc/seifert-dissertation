The 0D scaled model is verified using \gls{DNP} group parameters from thermal fission of $^{235}$U.
This verification uses the data from literature, introduced in Section \ref{sec:measurementofdnps}, combined and recreated here with the results from this work included.

Tables \ref{tab:macro-yields-net}, \ref{tab:macro-yield-delta-net}, \ref{tab:macro-half-lives-delta-net}, and \ref{tab:macro-half-lives-delta-net} show the \gls{DNP} group parameters from literature and how they compare to the 0D scaled model \gls{DNP} group parameters.
As previously discussed, because the 0D scaled model does not include the pulse irradiation data, the shorter-lived groups are expected to be less accurate.
This can be seen in the yields, where the groups 5 and 6 yields are significantly different, while groups 1 and 2 are much more similar to results from the literature.
The group half-lives are similar to the literature values, though group three is an outlier.
This is again likely due to the pulse irradiation data missing, as it is noticeably longer than the other values in the literature.

Table \ref{tab:total-delnu-compare-net} shows how the total delayed neutron yield for 100 fissions compares between the literature and for the 0D scaled model.
The 0D scaled model yield is noticeably larger than the other values in the literature, but the raw data is also larger.
This indicates not an issue with the \gls{DNP} group parameter formulation, but rather, that the pulse irradiation data is necessary to capture the smaller yields of the shorter-lived \gls{DNP} groups.
This can be seen in Table \ref{tab:macro-yields-net}, where the group 5 and 6 yields are greatly over-valued relative to other \gls{DNP} group yields in the literature.
Using the modified 0D scaled model, where groups three through six use the Keepin et al. \gls{DNP} group parameters, results in a yield which is more consistent with the other values in the literature.
