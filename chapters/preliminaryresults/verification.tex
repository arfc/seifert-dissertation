The 0D scaled model verification uses \gls{DNP} group parameters from thermal fission of $^{235}$U.
The first verification of interest is calculation of the delayed neutron yield $\bar{\nu}_{d}$ and average half-life $\bar{\tau}$, shown in Table \ref{tab:indiv-dnp-params}.
These values are calculated and then compared with the six group \gls{DNP} group parameters formulated from those \glspl{DNP}.
Ideally, these values would be identical, but the six-group fit cannot fully capture all decaying \glspl{DNP} behavior, producing small differences.
These differences are small relative to the uncertainty bounds for each term, which verifies that the group fit is accurately capturing the general behavior of the \glspl{DNP}.

Verification with the data from literature, introduced in Section \ref{sec:measurementofdnps}, is combined and recreated here with this work’s results.
Tables \ref{tab:macro-yields-net}, \ref{tab:macro-yield-delta-net}, \ref{tab:macro-half-lives-delta-net}, and \ref{tab:macro-half-lives-delta-net} show the \gls{DNP} group parameters from literature and how they compare to the 0D scaled model \gls{DNP} group parameters.
As discussed, the 0D scaled model excludes pulse irradiation data, so shorter-lived groups are less accurate.
This appears in the yields: groups five and six differ significantly, while groups one and two match the literature more closely.
The group half-lives generally agree with the literature, though groups three and five are outliers.
This likely arises from missing pulse irradiation data, as both group half-lives are approximately twice as large as the values in the literature.

Table \ref{tab:total-delnu-compare-net} compares the total delayed neutron yield for 100 fissions between the literature and the 0D scaled model.
It can be seen that the data is not within uncertainty bounds of each other, and even differs between different approaches for the same dataset.
Differences between microscopic and macroscopic delayed yields occur for many fissile nuclides, not only $^{235}$U \cite{mathieu2012new}.
The 0D scaled model yield exceeds other literature values, but this is a data issue and not a fitting issue. 
The summed \gls{DNP} data gives approximately the same yield as shown in Table \ref{tab:indiv-dnp-params}.
This indicates no issue with \gls{DNP} group parameter formulation; rather, pulse irradiation data is needed to capture smaller yields of shorter-lived \gls{DNP} groups.
This can be seen in Table \ref{tab:macro-yields-net}, where the group 5 and 6 yields are greatly over-valued relative to other \gls{DNP} group yields in the literature.
The modified 0D scaled model, using Keepin et al. \gls{DNP} group parameters for groups three through six, produces a yield more consistent with literature values.

Table \ref{tab:avg-halflife-net} compares average half-lives between the 0D scaled model and the literature.
The 0D scaled model average half-life is too short, primarily due to overvalued group five and six yields that give those half-lives greater weight.
The modified 0D scaled model, with groups three through six replaced by Keepin et al. \gls{DNP} group parameters, aligns much more closely with literature.
This again indicates the importance of pulse irradiation data to capture short-lived \gls{DNP} behavior.
