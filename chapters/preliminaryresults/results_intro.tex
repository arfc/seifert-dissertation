The preliminary results use the lowest-fidelity 0D scaled model to generate improved \gls{DNP} group parameters.
The results verify the \gls{DNP} group parameters using stationary thermal fission of $^{235}$U due to the abundance of data in the literature for this case.
These preliminary results investigate individual \glspl{DNP}, \gls{DNP} group parameter sensitivities, and \gls{DNP} group parameter verification.

Of the \gls{MSR} physical phenomena considered, only chemical removal has an impact on the 0D scaled model.
Varying the in-core and ex-core residence times does not affect the 0D scaled model because the scaling factor of the fission rate is identical to the scaling factor of the \gls{DNP} generation rate.
Because no decay chains are considered, the in-core and ex-core residence times do not have any effect.
The proposed 0D flow model will be able to model the effects of residence time, as it accounts for time-dependent decays and parasitic absorptions.
Although the 0D scaled model is rapid and enables more simulations, it does not incorporate fuel salt circulation into improved \gls{DNP} group parameter generation.

An additional consideration is that the pulse irradiation results are not included.
Whether combined via simultaneous solution of a larger matrix or solved separately and then combined, pulse irradiation results improve \gls{DNP} group parameters for shorter-lived groups \cite{keepin1957delayed}.
A simple investigation into the effects of pulse and saturation data is given in Appendix \ref{sec:appendixD}.
Parameters formulated for longer-lived groups are expected to be more accurate than for shorter-lived groups in the 0D scaled model, as it excludes pulse irradiation results.
The 0D scaled model cannot model pulse irradiations because the cumulative fission yields do not provide accurate concentrations for non-equilibrium concentrations.
Section \ref{sec:irrad-combine} discusses this further.
