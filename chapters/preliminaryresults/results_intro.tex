The preliminary results use the lowest-fidelity 0D scaled model to generate improved \gls{DNP} group parameters.
The results verify the \gls{DNP} group parameters using stationary thermal fission of $^{235}$U due to the abundance of data in the literature for this case.
These preliminary results investigate individual \glspl{DNP}, \gls{DNP} group parameter sensitivities, and \gls{DNP} group parameter verification.

Of the \gls{MSR} physical phenomena considered, only chemical removal has an impact on the 0D scaled model.
In-core and ex-core residence times do not change delayed neutron yield per fission because the number of fission events producing that count rate also varies.
In the simplified 0D scaled model, each \gls{DNP} source scales linearly at the same rate as the number of fissions.
The proposed 0D flow model will not have this linear response, as it accounts for time-dependent decays and parasitic absorptions.

Specifically, for the scaled flux method applied to the cumulative fission yield, the scaling term for both is the same.
Although this approach is rapid and enables more simulations, it does not incorporate fuel salt circulation into improved \gls{DNP} group parameter generation.
Approximate measured in-core and ex-core residence times for the \gls{MSRE} are 9 seconds and 16 seconds, respectively \cite{hanauer1967neutron}.

An additional consideration is that the pulse irradiation results are not included.
Whether combined via simultaneous solution of a larger matrix or solved separately and then combined, pulse irradiation results improve \gls{DNP} group parameters for shorter-lived groups \cite{keepin1957delayed}.
A simple investigation into the effects of pulse and saturation data is given in Appendix \ref{sec:appendixD}.
Longer-lived groups are expected to be more accurate than shorter-lived groups in the 0D scaled model, as it excludes pulse irradiation results.
Section \ref{sec:irrad-combine} discusses this further.

The row labeled "sampled simulations" shows the number of non-linear least squares solves used in the Monte Carlo sampling.
Each sampled simulation uses emission probabilities, half-lives, and fission yields within uncertainties from a normal distribution based on the nuclear data.
