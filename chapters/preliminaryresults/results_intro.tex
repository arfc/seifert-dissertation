The preliminary results use the lowest fidelity model, the 0D scaled model, to generate improved \gls{DNP} group parameters.
Specifically, thermal fission of $^{235}$U is used, since there is sufficient data in the literature to verify that the stationary case with no chemical removal produces expected results.
These preliminary results investigate individual \glspl{DNP}, the sensitivity of the \gls{DNP} group parameters to different variables, and verification of the \gls{DNP} group parameters.

Of the \gls{MSR} physical phenomena considered, only the chemical removal has an impact on the 0D scaled model.
The in-core and ex-core residence times do not change the delayed neutron yield per fission because the number of fission events that lead to that count rate also varies.
Due to the simplification of the 0D scaled model, the source of each \gls{DNP} scales at the same rate as the number of fissions linearly.
The proposed 0D flow model will not have this linear response, as it will account for time-dependent decays and parasitic absorptions.

Specifically, for the scaled flux method applied to the cumulative fission yield, the scaling term for both is the same.
Although this approach is rapid and enables more simulations to be executed, it also does not allow for the impact of circulation of the fuel salt to be incorporated into the improved \gls{DNP} group parameter generation.
For the \gls{MSRE}, approximate measured residence times for the in-core and ex-core regions are 9 seconds and 16 seconds, respectively \cite{hanauer1967neutron}.

An additional consideration for these preliminary results is that pulse irradiation results are not included.
Whether the results from the pulse irradiation simulation are combined via simultaneous solution of a larger matrix or separately solved and then combined, they improve the \gls{DNP} group parameters for the shorter-lived groups \cite{keepin1957delayed}.
This means that the longer-lived groups can be expected to be more accurate than the shorter-lived groups for the 0D scaled model, as it does not include pulse irradiation results.
This is discussed further in Section \ref{sec:irrad-combine}.

These results, unless otherwise specified, use the parameters given in Table \ref{tab:default-0dsacled}.
The sampled simulations row indicates the number of non-linear least squares solves that generate \gls{DNP} group parameters.
For each sampled simulation, the emission probability, half-lives, and fission yields are sampled within their uncertainties using a normal distribution.
